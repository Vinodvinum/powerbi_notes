Chapter 1 — What is BI & Power BI

BI = Business Intelligence — using data to make better business decisions. Power BI is a tool to create reports and dashboards from data. Focus on showing totals, top categories, and trends rather than raw rows.

Quick example 
order_id\tamount\tcity
1\t5000\tBangalore
2\t3000\tMysore

Insight: Total Sales → ₹8000, Top City → Bangalore
Visual

Notes: Show a single-card KPI (Total Sales), a bar for Top Cities and a small trend chart for time.

Relationships (joins), star schema, fact vs dimension tables. Keep granularity consistent and avoid many-to-many unless required.

Bar, line, pie, and table visuals. Choose visuals based on story: trend = line, composition = stacked bar, top N = bar.

Chapter 8 — Advanced Visuals

Chapter 9 — Dashboard Design

Layout, color usage, and business storytelling. Focus on the end-user question and place the most important KPI top-left.

Chapter 10 — What is DAX

Difference between calculated columns and measures. Measures are evaluated at query time and are the preferred way to compute aggregations.

Example measure:
Total Sales = SUM(fact_sales[amount])

Filtered example:
Sales Last Year = CALCULATE([Total Sales], SAMEPERIODLASTYEAR(Date[Date]))
